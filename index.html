№ 001

Kent State Instagram

Social media auditColor contrastAlt text

Light text on a gold background: 1.6:1 contrast. The minimum is 4.5:1.

5Posts audited
4 of 5Posts with key info only in the image
1.6:1Lowest contrast. Minimum is 4.5:1

The ask

Audit five Kent State Athletics posts the way a screen reader user, a low-vision fan, and a scroller with images off would meet them.

What I found

  • Alt text was auto-generated and said nothing useful
  • The final score, event time, and full schedule lived only inside the graphics
  • A whole official statement was set as an image of text
  • Game day details sat on gold at 1.6:1. The same text on navy hit 10.88:1

The fix

  • Write real alt text: the player, jersey #8, mid-game, and the 76–75 final score
  • Put the score, time, and schedule in the caption too
  • Solid panel behind event details, 4.5:1 minimum

№ 002

Temu

Cognitive designAttentionMemory load

Engineered to grab attention. Not engineered to help you find anything.

NonstopPop-ups, countdown timers, and coupon wheels fighting for attention
Tier 3Rewards level reached with zero explanation
1¢Deal on items worth $150+. Trust: gone

The ask

Run Temu through cognitive design: attention, perception, memory, learning, and decision-making.

What I found

  • Countdown timers and lightning deals work, for impulse buys
  • Banners and pop-ups compete with no hierarchy, so everything shouts
  • Top menu is plain text with no cue that it is tappable
  • Stacked coupons and rewards overload memory and erode trust
  • Too many options, too few filters: decision paralysis

The fix

  • Declutter: real visual hierarchy and whitespace
  • One “My Offers” hub listing every active coupon
  • Filters that actually narrow the catalog

№ 003

Palmstreet

Cognitive designGestaltSignifiers

Restraint as a feature: calm grid, clear categories, one mystery button.

CalmClean grid, clear categories, no pushy pop-ups
+Bright green button with no label. Sell? Post? Go live?
GoneNo Recently Viewed, so finding an item again means starting over

The ask

The counterpoint to Temu: a niche live-selling marketplace for plants, collectibles, and more, judged on the same cognitive lens.

What I found

  • Clean, evenly spaced grid. Gestalt similarity does the sorting
  • Emoji category buttons with labels: fast to recognize
  • Referral gift icon sits quietly by search, present but not pushy
  • The bright green + button never says what it does
  • No “Recently Viewed,” so finding an item again means starting over

The fix

  • Label the + (“Sell” or “Go live”)
  • Tooltips on the + and gift box for first-timers
  • Add “Recently Viewed”

№ 004

Planta

Norman’s principlesFeedbackConstraints

Great at reminding you. No way back when you tap the wrong plant.

RedOverdue tasks flagged at a glance
No undoMark the wrong plant done and it vanishes, no history
App-onlyPlant sitters must download Planta just to help

The ask

Apply Norman’s principles to an AI plant care app: affordances, signifiers, constraints, mapping, feedback.

What I found

  • Red overdue tasks are a clear signifier
  • The stethoscope icon maps straight to the Dr. Planta diagnosis tool
  • Light is set per site, not per plant, so people look in the wrong place
  • No vacation mode. A plant sitter has to download the app
  • Tasks marked done by accident vanish, no history, no undo

The fix

  • Tooltip or link from a plant to its site’s light settings
  • Vacation mode, or share care steps by text or email
  • Task history plus an undo

    Portland Leather

    Norman’s principlesE-commerceTrust

    Sale price on the button. Full price in the cart.

    $92.40Sale price shown on the product page
    $132.00What the cart charges, unless you find the coupon
    ★ vs ♥Two nav icons that look like the same job

    The ask

    Same Norman lens on a brand new app (launched Oct 2025) from a brand with a decade on the web.

    What I found

    • Persistent bottom nav and category tabs mirror the website
    • Live color swatches and clearly marked checkout fields
    • Sale price shows in red above Add to Cart, but the app does not apply it. The website does
    • A star and a heart both sit in the nav. Which one saves?
    • Small feedback delays and basic filters, normal for year one

    The fix

    • Auto-apply the promo, like the website
    • Give the star and heart distinct jobs, or move rewards to the profile
    • Sharper filters

    № 006

    Wedding Seating App

    Cognitive design flowsFeature Integration TheoryGood vs. bad

    Same task, two designs. One catches the vegan guest assigned chicken. One doesn’t.

    TomVegan guest assigned chicken. The bad design hides it
    13 → 2Tables shown vs. tables actually open
    4 × 4Norman principles broken, each tied to a cognitive construct

    The ask

    Design a good and a deliberately bad interaction flow for a wedding seating tool, then prove why the bad one fails using cognitive psychology.

    What I found

    • Visibility × whole before parts: a flat dashboard buries the plus-ones task
    • Constraint × conjunction search: 13 identical table buttons instead of the 2 that are open
    • Affordance × two visual pathways: data you can read but no Confirm to act on
    • Feedback × Feature Integration Theory: no flag, so vegan + chicken blends in

    Why it matters

    • A single pop-out feature (a flag) is seen automatically
    • A conflict that needs two facts combined needs focused attention. People miss it
    • Bad design here sends the wrong meal to the caterer
