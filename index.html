The Meatball Project

MEATBALL // GRADUATE STUDENT FINAL PROJECT ● IN PROGRESS
🍝

Swipe together. Eat together.

Meatball

A graduate student final project addressing one of the harder questions a group has to answer together: what to eat. Built for couples and small groups who face it every night. In my survey of 111 people, 56% negotiate what to eat at least a few times a week, and decision fatigue was the #1 pain point (42%).

Grounded in the Abilene Paradox: the group dynamic where everyone ends up at a choice nobody actually wanted, because each person assumed someone else preferred it. Original brand, naming, and product positioning developed from the ground up.

Greenhouse Shelf Remedy

3D printedIterationPhysical product

A sagging acrylic shelf, two glass doors, and several iterations.

0Cabinet modifications
2Brackets, one per door
3Plate versions, plus a new slope shelf support

TL;DR

9×Easily holds 9× the weight, with less sag than 1 plant on the old supports. 10 lb 13 oz wasn't the limit. The new back support carries the shelf even with both doors open, and a slope shelf support on each door levels the front when they close.
54 → 50Measure the real thing, not a photo
25°Design for the loaded shelf, not the empty one

The problem

The wide acrylic shelf in my IKEA Milsbo greenhouse sits on top of 5 small supports, one at each corner and one at the center back. Loaded with watered pots, it sags along the front. I skipped drilling: every shelf in the cabinet is adjustable, and today's fix could be tomorrow's problem.

Small dip⚖️ 1 lb 3.9 oz
Bigger dip⚖️ 3 lb 13.6 oz
Biggest dip⚖️ 6 lb 2.1 oz
Back support: less dip⚖️ 10 lb 13 oz · +4 lb 11 oz
Both upgrades: nearly flat⚖️ 10 lb 13 oz · +4 lb 11 oz

Starting point

  • Idea: Brad_Reputation's design (opens in new tab) (CC BY). The shelf rides up a slope as the door closes
  • His: super-glued at one height, with a steep front a heavy, sagging shelf can't get under
  • Mine: adjustable with a door plate, peg rail, and pegs, plus a gentler slope for a loaded shelf

Why both doors

  • Open one door to water, the other still holds the shelf
  • Two contact points split the load across the front
  • The same part works left and right

WATCH IT WORK

WHAT CHANGED

  1. V1
    Plate + peg rail. The plate sits under both cabinet screws, with 7 peg holes to move the slope shelf support up or down based on how much weight the shelf holds. I assumed the screws were 54 mm apart; calipers said 50, so the bottom hole missed by 4 mm.
  2. V2
    Swapped that screw for a pin. I needed the slope shelf support lower, which covered the bottom screw spot. Held by only the top screw, the plate could shift left and right, so I tried a pin in the bottom hole and added an 8th peg hole. I dropped the pin to keep the door handle secure.
  3. V3
    Screw back, pocket over its head. An 11.5 mm pocket on the back fits over the bottom screw, so the plate can't swing. Back to 7 peg holes, because they're bigger now and 9 mm apart to match the slope shelf support's pegs.
  4. SLOPE
    Gentler slope shelf support. Loaded with pots, the shelf sagged below the old slope shelf support's blunt front and caught on it. The new one climbs at 25° to a thin tip 10 mm low, so the shelf slides on instead.
  5. BACK
    New wider back support. Replaces the small stock support at the middle back of the cabinet. It carries the load when both doors are open, so the shelf never depends on the doors. Worked first try.

Cocktail Card Sort

Open card sortInformation architectureTry it yourself

Nine people agreed almost perfectly on which cards belonged together. Then they gave those groups 25 different names.

Card Sort Challenge · Cocktails

0 / 12

Which pile does this belong in?

Drag it onto a pile · or press the number beside it

Tap any card sitting in a pile to pull it back out.

For an IA study I picked a real site to pull apart: MakeMeACocktail.com. It already finds you drinks from the ingredients you have on hand, but not from the flavor you're after, the mood you're in, or the barware actually in your drawer.

So I wrote 20 cards for those three proposed categories and ran a card sort with 9 participants in Lyssna. You're getting 12 of those cards here, enough to see what happened without making you sort the lot.

There are no labels on the piles, exactly like the open sort my participants did. Group the cards however they make sense to you, then name what you built.

Two ways to place a card. Pick whichever you like.

  • Drag Pick the card up and drop it on a pile.
  • Keys Fastest way through.

Changed your mind? Undo last card, press Backspace, or tap any card already sitting in a pile to pull it back out. Nothing is locked in until you say so.

How you did

How your names did

Nobody could agree what to call these piles.

My 9 participants got no labels either. They grouped the cards, made as many piles as they wanted, and wrote their own name on each one. Added up, they invented 25 different category names.

Only three of those names were used by more than one person:

  • 5 people said Tools
  • 4 people said Taste
  • 3 people said Flavors

Every other name was used once and once only. Mood. Vibes. Theme. Emotions. Bar Equipment. Drink Making Items.

That's the finding. They put the same cards in the same piles. Then they called those piles 25 different things.

A card sort doesn't hand you the architecture. It hands you the vocabulary, and the vocabulary is where the argument lives.

№ 002

Kent State Instagram

Social media auditColor contrastAlt text

Light text on a gold background: 1.6:1 contrast. The minimum is 4.5:1.

5Posts audited
4 of 5Posts with key info only in the image
1.6:1Lowest contrast. Minimum is 4.5:1

The ask

Audit five Kent State Athletics posts the way a screen reader user, a low-vision fan, and a scroller with images off would meet them.

What I found

  • Alt text was auto-generated and said nothing useful
  • The final score, event time, and full schedule lived only inside the graphics
  • A whole official statement was set as an image of text
  • Game day details sat on gold at 1.6:1. The same text on navy hit 10.88:1

The fix

  • Write real alt text: the player, jersey #8, mid-game, and the 76–75 final score
  • Put the score, time, and schedule in the caption too
  • Solid panel behind event details, 4.5:1 minimum

Temu

Cognitive designAttentionMemory load

Engineered to grab attention. Not engineered to help you find anything.

NonstopPop-ups, countdown timers, and coupon wheels fighting for attention
Tier 3Rewards level reached with zero explanation
1¢Deal on items worth $150+. Trust: gone

The ask

Run Temu through cognitive design: attention, perception, memory, learning, and decision-making.

What I found

  • Countdown timers and lightning deals work, for impulse buys
  • Banners and pop-ups compete with no hierarchy, so everything shouts
  • Top menu is plain text with no cue that it is tappable
  • Stacked coupons and rewards overload memory and erode trust
  • Too many options, too few filters: decision paralysis

The fix

  • Declutter: real visual hierarchy and whitespace
  • One “My Offers” hub listing every active coupon
  • Filters that actually narrow the catalog

Palmstreet

Cognitive designGestaltSignifiers

Restraint as a feature: calm grid, clear categories, one mystery button.

CalmClean grid, clear categories, no pushy pop-ups
+Bright green button with no label. Sell? Post? Go live?
0No Recently Viewed, so finding an item again means starting over

The ask

The counterpoint to Temu: a niche live-selling marketplace for plants, collectibles, and more, judged on the same cognitive lens.

What I found

  • Clean, evenly spaced grid. Gestalt similarity does the sorting
  • Emoji category buttons with labels: fast to recognize
  • Referral gift icon sits quietly by search, present but not pushy
  • The bright green + button never says what it does
  • No “Recently Viewed,” so finding an item again means starting over

The fix

  • Label the + (“Sell” or “Post”)
  • Tooltips on the + and gift box for first-timers
  • Add “Recently Viewed”

№ 005

Planta

Norman’s principlesFeedbackConstraints

Great at reminding you. No way back when you tap the wrong plant.

RedOverdue tasks flagged at a glance
No undoMark the wrong plant done and it vanishes, no history
App-onlyPlant sitters must download Planta just to help

The ask

Apply Norman’s principles to an AI plant care app: affordances, signifiers, constraints, mapping, feedback.

What I found

  • Red overdue tasks are a clear signifier
  • The stethoscope icon maps straight to the Dr. Planta diagnosis tool
  • Light is set per site, not per plant, so people look in the wrong place
  • No vacation mode. A plant sitter has to download the app
  • Tasks marked done by accident vanish, no history, no undo

The fix

  • Tooltip or link from a plant to its site’s light settings
  • Vacation mode, or share care steps by text or email
  • Task history plus an undo

    Sale price on the button. Full price in the cart.

    The ask

    Same Norman lens on a brand new app (launched Oct 2025) from a brand with a decade on the web.

    What I found

    • Persistent bottom nav and category tabs mirror the website
    • Live color swatches and clearly marked checkout fields

    № 007

    Wedding Seating App

    Cognitive design flowsFeature Integration TheoryGood vs. bad

    Same task, two designs. One catches the vegan guest assigned chicken. One doesn’t.

    TomVegan guest assigned chicken. The bad design hides it
    13 → 2Tables shown vs. tables that fit the seating rules
    4 + 44 Norman principles broken, 4 cognitive constructs

    The ask

    Design a good and a deliberately bad interaction flow for a wedding seating tool, then prove why the bad one fails using cognitive psychology.

    What I found

    • Visibility × whole before parts: a flat dashboard buries the plus-ones task
    • Constraint × conjunction search: 13 identical table buttons instead of the 2 that fit the rules
    • Affordance × two visual pathways: data you can read but no Confirm to act on
    • Attention × Feature Integration Theory: no flag, so vegan + chicken blends in

    Why it matters

    • A single pop-out feature (a flag) is seen automatically
    • A conflict that needs two facts combined needs focused attention. People miss it
    • Bad design here sends the wrong meal to the caterer
